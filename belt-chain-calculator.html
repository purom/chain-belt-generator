Assembly · true size

Method. Exact open-belt geometry on the pitch line: L = 2√(C² − (R−r)²) + π(R+r) + 2(R−r)·asin((R−r)/C), solved numerically for C. Pitch Ø = teeth × pitch / π. Outside Ø = pitch Ø − 2 × pitch-line differential.

DXF. Groove = round bottom + tapered flanks + tip fillets, fitted to the groove shapes of the widely used open-source parametric pulley script (within ~0.015 mm for HTD 5M and GT2 2mm; HTD 3M and GT2 3mm scaled to their published depth and width). MXL, XL, L and H (ANSI / RMA inch pitch) use the ISO 5294 trapezoidal groove: 40° flanks, flat bottom with corner radii, and ISO 5296 belt teeth; their part numbers follow the inch convention, e.g. 130XL025 = 13.0 in long, 0.25 in wide. Belt teeth = the groove shape with tip clearance and ~4 % of the pitch flank clearance, wrapped on the pulley OD and straight on the spans; assembly exports turn each pulley so its grooves sit under the belt teeth. Exported as R12 ASCII DXF in millimetres with true arcs (polyline bulges). Test-fit a printed or cut pulley before relying on it under load.

Teeth-in-mesh and minimum-pulley warnings are rules of thumb — check your belt maker's design guide for load ratings.

Method. Chain length on the pitch circles: L = 2√(C² − (R₂−R₁)²)/p + [z₁(π−2φ) + z₂(π+2φ)]/2π links, φ = asin((R₂−R₁)/C), solved numerically for C. Pitch Ø = p / sin(180°/z). Each tooth of wrap counts as one pitch.

Sprockets. ANSI B29.1 standard tooth form (seating, working and topping curves), outside Ø = p(0.6 + cot 180°/z), as true arcs in the DXF. Rollers can enter and leave the gaps without touching the teeth. Test-fit a printed or cut sprocket before relying on it under load.

Chain data. ANSI / ISO 606 A series and ISO 606 B series from the standard and maker tables; motorcycle and bicycle chains use typical maker values. Exports (DXF side views, STEP solids) put every link exactly one pitch long — any slack sits in the lower span. STEP is AP214 in millimetres, one solid per plate, roller, bushing and pin.
